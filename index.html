01 · What you actually do

Four jobs. Done by a single pilot. You.

No autopilot, no arcade physics. Awake to a crippled reactor. Learn and adapt quickly, or fail and get forgotten forever.

Budget the power

A crippled reactor, two buses, eighteen systems that all want to be on. The grid sheds by priority, not by fairness. Handle a realistic power grid to power what really is important, at the moment.

Plan the burn

Real Newtonian-Kepler physics on a solar map drawn to scale. Nothing solves the transfer for you. You aim, burn and you must live with your calculations, bad or good. Scavenge for resources, explore stations and derelicts.

Sleep through it

Transits take weeks. Put the crew back in the pod and set what will wake you. Realistic pilot aging, the solar system does not stop while you sleep.

Fly the last kilometre

Close quarters happen in manual flight. Hand-fly the approach, match the station's spin, hold seven gates until the clamp takes.

03 · Under the hood

Realistic scale solar system, with simulated physics and proc gen events.

Every readout is backed by something actually running. The numbers are not set dressing.

CREW

Lone pilot

You are a M.O.R.S.S. pilot. Crew is just you, the pilot, and your ship AI. You can go into hypersleep, while your ship auto-pilot handles some aspects, others you need to be awake. Real physiological simulation like life support, atmosphere and G's effects as aging.

PWR

Realistic power grid system

Two buses, five sources, one tie. Load shedding runs on priority, so the engine goes dark before life support does.

NAV

Kepler physics

Four nodes, one prediction line, no solved burns. Lose avionics and you lose the prediction, never the ability to burn blindly. You need to explore and fly to known locations in order to obtain navigation data, otherwise you fly blind directly to the void.

DOCK

Stations

Range, closure, offset, angle, rate, roll: seven gates, all inside tolerance, held. Docking a spinning collar means co-rotating with it. Go too fast and you may lose your ship and the station.

G-LOAD

Real stress to the crew and ship

No arcade physics or unrealistic flight mechanics. You can fly outside the ship envelope but it will fatigue and may snap due to over g forces, as the pilot. Realistic physiological effects as G-LOC, A-LOC, greyout redout, etc.

PLANETS

Entry burns what it touches

Heat flux goes as velocity cubed. Come in fast and the airflow strips modules off the leading face. There is an altitude past which you cannot pull up. Your ship was not designed to atmosphere re-entry.

ARCHIVE

A history that was simulated

155 years of backstory generated per seed, rendered into the logs you pull out of dead stations' mainframes. Also, a multi layered proc gen system that will pace the solar system throughout 300 years.

SOI

Thirty-two bodies, real distances

Solar system with realistic scale, laid out under one gravity law. The distances are not a backdrop, they are part of the problem.
